# WiseLanka methodology and evaluation design — critical audit

**Purpose and status.** This is a read-only audit of the existing methodology, proposal/interim baseline, evaluation materials and saved technical evidence. It does not rewrite Chapter 3, authorize collection or verify the institutional ethics decision. **There are zero genuine participant responses.** Synthetic profiles, the nine-response reporting demonstration, temporary Phase 4 end-to-end records, test submissions and blank judgement sheets are not participant research data or research findings.

## 1. Methodology actually described

The approved proposal specifies Design Science Research (DSR), Agile-inspired iterative prototyping, a structured educational knowledge base, rule-based eligibility with lightweight AI assistance, and functional, scenario, explanation, accessibility and usability evaluation. Chapter 3 retains that baseline and describes an artifact-building project rather than an experiment whose outcomes have been established. The August interim report calls the orientation pragmatic and describes requirements translated deductively into rules. The current chapter mentions those interim labels, but does not fully reconcile them into a single, explicit account of research philosophy, reasoning, methodology, and software development approach.

These terms should not be treated as interchangeable. DSR is the research methodology used to design, demonstrate and evaluate an artifact; Agile-inspired iteration is the development approach; deduction describes one way requirements were translated into rules. A pragmatic orientation may be compatible with an applied artifact study, but the report should justify that position and explain where evidence was also inspected, revised or interpreted inductively. Merely naming “pragmatism”, “deduction”, “DSR” and “Agile” is not sufficient. The report should also state whether it actually used mixed methods. At present, human qualitative and questionnaire procedures are prepared, not conducted; it is therefore inaccurate to describe the project as having completed a mixed-methods user evaluation.

Hevner et al. (2004) and Peffers et al. (2007) provide a plausible DSR foundation, and the current chapter usefully warns that its retrospective DSR table is reconstructed from work-package evidence. The methodology still needs a stronger explanation of why DSR fits this problem, how relevance/rigor/design cycles were operationalised, what knowledge beyond code construction is claimed, and how evaluation findings caused design decisions. Chapter 3 records a concrete WP5 source-correction cycle and accessibility repairs, which is stronger evidence of iterative evaluation than a generic Agile label. Keep the claim proportionate: work-package milestones are not automatically a prospectively documented research diary. The Hevner source access recorded in the reference register is abstract/metadata level; the student should inspect the primary paper before relying on detailed framework claims.

## 2. Activity-by-activity methodological assessment

### Requirements, learner context and source analysis

The approved proposal and interim report provide a traceable requirements baseline, and the current work maps the eight objectives and FR1–FR9 to implementation and evidence. This is document analysis and requirements engineering, not direct investigation of current learner challenges. Historical Sri Lankan guidance and child digital-access sources can inform context but cannot substitute for current evidence from A/L, vocational and early-career target users. No user interviews, survey or focus group has been conducted. Accordingly, Objective II and claims about present user needs remain only partly evidenced.

Educational-source work is more substantial than a code-only test: Sprint 2A froze the CSV snapshot, source records and scenario oracle, and source-grounded expected outputs were authored before the application run. The 45-case baseline recorded 42 agreements and three disagreements. Sprint 2A.1 preserved that baseline and ran a controlled correction/replay, reporting 43 agreements and two remaining input limitations with no unexpected change to the other 44 cases. The evaluator was also the implementer and had inspected the application; this is source-grounded evaluation, not independent educational adjudication. The evidence supports bounded review of the selected rules and missing-evidence semantics, not national admissions accuracy. Source page access, source-to-rule traceability, publication date and actual provider decision are different evidence questions. Current source freshness and incomplete vocational coverage remain threats.

### Knowledge base and policy-aware eligibility

The curated CSV knowledge base and its integrity checks provide reproducible artifact evidence. The verified snapshot covered 11 programmes across IT (7), Business and Management (1), and Engineering (3), together with selected qualifications, rule records, progression edges and source links. This is deliberately bounded and imbalanced, not a representative catalogue or evidence of demand. CSV structural validation can detect missing/duplicate identifiers and broken relationships; it cannot establish that every source statement is current, accurately interpreted, or applicable to a particular learner.

The deterministic eligibility engine is the policy authority. Its `MET`, `NOT_MET` and `NOT_ASSESSED` results are rule semantics, not probabilistic or human assessments. Tests can establish that code applies encoded conditions; the educational correctness protocol separately checks selected rules against official texts. The source-grounded cases were authored by the implementer, and some conditions (for example provider selection decisions, intake/year or missing learner evidence) cannot be resolved from the request. Therefore, `NOT_ASSESSED` is a defensible system response to insufficient evidence, but not proof that the knowledge base is complete. Do not combine source-verification counts, scenario agreement or test pass rates into an “eligibility accuracy” percentage.

### Ranking methods and recommendation relevance

The production comparison includes deterministic/grouped ordering, a TF–IDF/cosine baseline and frozen MiniLM semantic inference. Sprint 2B compared the methods over 21 synthetic scenarios, including nine development-exposed and 12 catalogue-aware follow-ups, with identical declared candidate sets and academic status partitions. It recorded 63 comparator rows and repeated runtime measurements. The follow-ups were frozen before observing results, but were catalogue-aware and are not independent held-out scenarios. They cannot be represented as an unbiased test set.

No independent relevance labels exist; nDCG, Precision@K, MAP, relevance accuracy and superiority were not computed. The observed rank-order changes, deterministic stability, fallback and latency are functional/operational evidence only. MiniLM is frozen pretrained inference, not a model trained on WiseLanka feedback; TF–IDF does not learn user preferences. Formal eligibility is kept separate from relevance in the design. The next relevance evaluation requires a reconciled, version-frozen, method-blinded judgement pack, independently obtained ratings, defined reviewer eligibility and a predeclared metric/analysis. Existing documents disagree on pack size/version: current Sprint 2B audit material describes 127 scenario–programme pairs per reviewer, while `independent-ranking/preparation-manifest.json` and the older protocol record nine cases/51 unique pairs per packet. Resolve this before issuing any cards. The 127 pairs are potential judgements, not people or completed ratings.

### Functional, scenario, performance and accessibility evaluation

Saved evidence records automated backend/browser suites, live API workflows, data integrity, a fresh-install check, synthetic scenarios, performance probes and automated accessibility scans. The WP5 snapshot reports 205 backend tests, 36 browser tests, five live workflows, 14 synthetic reference cases, and axe scans over nine states with zero detected violations for the selected rule set. Later Sprint 2A/2B reports add targeted educational-correctness and ranking experiments. These figures demonstrate tested behaviour in recorded environments; they do not show educational effectiveness, independent correctness, broad hardware compatibility or user satisfaction. Do not aggregate different snapshots or denominators as one research result.

Manual keyboard, screen-reader, high-zoom, real mobile, fluent Sinhala/Tamil, and native microphone checks remain pending in the saved accessibility checklist. Automated axe scans cover selected rendered states and WCAG 2.0/2.1 rules, not full WCAG 2.2 conformance. Browser speech tests are mocked/capability checks; they do not establish actual recognition performance, language support or local-only audio processing.

### Adult usability, comprehension and explanation evaluation

The proposal commits to usability feedback and explanation review, but these are **prepared, not conducted**. The proposed target of eight consenting adults (practical range 6–10 only if approved) is a small convenience/purposive exploratory sample, not a statistically powered or representative sample. It may identify task friction and comprehension problems if approved and executed carefully. It cannot support a population estimate, causal claim that WiseLanka improves outcomes, or generalisation across three learner categories. Actual recruitment channels, eligibility details, sample size, and category coverage require confirmation; they cannot be treated as approved merely because they appear in `EVALUATION_PLAN_v2.md`.

The questionnaire is custom, not a validated SUS instrument. It contains conditional task-context items, 1–5 agreement ratings, multiple-choice comprehension questions and optional written feedback. Q20–Q23 probe whether participants distinguish recorded requirements, missing evidence, conditional progression and official verification. These answers can assess specific interpretation, but correct answers may be guessed and do not establish durable understanding or trust. The main questionnaire has no validated trust scale; Q19 is perceived usefulness, and the separate v2 comparison addendum’s bounded-reliance items are proposed, unvalidated and not approved. Keep ratings, observed task outcomes and comprehension scores separate.

There is a material **task-version mismatch**. The participant questionnaire offers four task labels: Task 1 A/L profile, Task 2 career pathways, Task 3 O/L profile, Task 4 qualification progression. The moderator cards in `PARTICIPANT_INFORMATION_AND_CONSENT.md` and `TASKS_AND_EXPLANATION_RUBRIC.md` instead define six task labels: T1 O/L/foundation, T2 A/L/IT, T3 Business or failed Engineering, T4 NVQ progression, T5 skills/scholarship and T6 language/accessibility. Questionnaire conditionals such as Q6 (Task 1 A/L), Q7 (Task 3 O/L), Q16 (“O/L learner” for Task 4) and Q24 (Task 4 progression) can therefore be inapplicable or misleading under the six-card sequence. The v2 comparison plan adds a two-condition source-lookup comparison, not a single reconciled task list. Freeze one master task set, align labels and conditional applicability, and version all instruments before any study.

The planned v2 comparison is more defensible than a vague “normal search” condition because it defines a bounded official-link-sheet baseline, fixed sources, matched time and counterbalanced order. However, the participant uses different scenarios (S1 and S2) across the two conditions; domain/difficulty differences, learning/carryover, source-page change, moderator assistance and inability to blind the participant limit causal interpretation. With eight people, results should be descriptive paired observations only. A comparison can support “observed performance on these tasks under these procedures,” not a general claim of improvement over ordinary guidance.

## 3. Ethics, consent, observation and reporting

The proposal excludes under-18 participants and unnecessary sensitive educational information. The questionnaire’s age gate and fictional-profile instructions are consistent with that baseline. However, repository materials state that no participants have been recruited and no human results exist. The ethics submission receipt establishes submission, not clearance. The final signed decision/conditions and approved information sheet were not located; no collection should begin until their existence and applicability are verified.

The existing ethics alignment record flags unresolved differences between the submitted form and proposed procedure: observation was marked N/A while the new procedure records task completion/time/errors/help; optional omission was marked NO while the current questionnaire lets respondents skip questions; confidentiality was marked N/A while the plan describes coded/pseudonymised data; and debriefing was marked NO while newer documents propose it. The original form also refers to speech-to-text/NLP, whereas the current standard session excludes native voice. These may require supervisor clarification or an approved amendment; do not silently reinterpret the submitted form. Approval is also needed for the independent ranking-review track, recruitment/sample, comparison tasks, instrument versions and translated materials.

The Phase 4 research system is a technical collection workflow, not authorization to collect. Its normal collection switch defaults disabled; the end-to-end workflow was tested only with synthetic data in a temporary database. Invitation/session identifiers and observation linking are implemented, while the consent flag is an operator attestation. There is no production researcher authentication/dashboard, public deployment, TLS/data-at-rest assurance, managed backup, retention/deletion automation or withdrawal portal. The reporting tool is local/read-only; its synthetic demonstration outputs must not be confused with real responses. This audit does not inspect or alter a real research database. The user-provided study status is zero genuine participant responses.

## 6. Principal validity threats

- **Construct validity:** similarity, qualification status, perceived usefulness, self-reported trust and comprehension are different constructs. Questionnaire ratings alone cannot demonstrate understanding or educational suitability.
- **Internal validity:** implementer-authored scenarios/oracles, moderator knowledge, assistance, participants seeing the condition, unequal tasks and carryover could shape observed outcomes. Independent judgements reduce label circularity but do not make catalogue-aware scenarios independent.
- **External validity:** 11 programmes, three uneven domains, selected providers, one local machine/browser configuration, synthetic learner profiles and a small convenience sample sharply limit generalisation.
- **Data/policy validity:** official source access is not independent policy adjudication; programme pages and policies can change by intake; published minimums may omit selection/provider decisions; no model can resolve absent evidence.
- **Ranking validity:** no independent relevance labels, no trained feedback data and no independent held-out cases. Observed order changes cannot establish improvement.
- **Measurement bias:** custom, positively framed items are susceptible to acquiescence, recall and demand effects; self-report trust/usefulness may diverge from correct interpretation and behaviour. No SUS score exists.
- **Accessibility validity:** automated scans and mocked speech cannot establish real access for screen-reader users, mobile contexts, Sinhala/Tamil speakers or native speech-recognition use.
- **Ethics/data validity:** unclear clearance scope and unresolved consent-form differences block human collection; small-sample comments may be indirectly identifiable even without names.

## 7. Priority improvements before revising Chapter 3

1. Obtain and verify the actual ethics decision/conditions and resolve B4–B7 differences in writing. Until then, recruitment, pilot sessions and relevance judgements remain blocked.
2. Reconcile and freeze one master research protocol: task list/labels, questionnaire conditionals, instrument versions, sequence allocation, source baseline and ranking-pack count/hash. Correct documents only after approval decisions are known.
3. Clarify the methodology chain: pragmatic orientation (if retained), DSR rationale and cycles, deductive/inductive reasoning, Agile-inspired development, and the exact status of human research. Support method claims with sources the student has read.
4. Replace the stale Chapter 3 case counts with a dated evidence chronology: 45-case educational baseline and controlled replay, 21-case comparator experiment, completed software/source checks, and **no** relevance labels or participant outcomes. Preserve earlier snapshots and their denominators.
5. Strengthen the adult-study plan as exploratory: specify approved sampling/recruitment, task burden, objective scoring rubric, moderator script, missingness/withdrawal handling, descriptive analysis and qualitative coding. Avoid causal or representativeness language.
6. Reconcile questionnaire domains with approved research questions: explicitly separate usefulness, reliance/trust, correctness and comprehension; add or revise any trust measure only through supervisor/ethics approval. Do not call the custom instrument validated or SUS.
7. Keep separate evidence streams in results: source correctness, rule-engine behavior, ranking relevance, response-time/resource measurements, accessibility conformance checks, user tasks and self-reported perceptions. No composite “accuracy” score.
8. The student should maintain a genuine dated research diary and reflection on failures, source corrections, protocol deviations and researcher role; do not reconstruct missing diary entries after the fact.

**Audit conclusion:** DSR is a plausible methodology for this artifact-led project, and the technical evaluation has meaningful, reproducible components. The present methodological account is not yet a complete evaluation design: human evaluation and independent relevance assessment are unconducted, the task/instrument versions conflict, and ethics clearance is not verified. Chapter 3 should be revised only after the protocol and ethics questions are resolved; until then, all human-research activity remains pending.
