# WiseLanka Literature Evidence Matrix — Stage 1

**Purpose:** verified source appraisal and critical comparison to inform a later revision of Chapter 2. This is research documentation, not a replacement literature review. Chapter 2 has not been changed.

**Project baseline:** the approved aim and eight objectives remain unchanged. The matrix is particularly relevant to Objectives I, II, III, IV, V, VI and VII and the main research question and supporting questions I–V. Relevance to WiseLanka is an analytical mapping by the reviewer; it is not a finding made by the cited source.

**Verification rule:** bibliographic identity, availability of a text, depth actually examined, source-supported findings and transfer to WiseLanka are separate judgements. “Full text examined” means the cited article/report/standard text was available and its relevant methods/results/limitations were inspected; it does not mean every page was exhaustively appraised. No unavailable method, sample, measure or result has been inferred. For conceptual works and standards, dataset/sample/evaluation are marked not applicable.

## Critical comparison and source disagreements

| Comparison | What the evidence supports | Difference, contradiction or limit | Defensible WiseLanka inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning-resource recommender reviews vs major/career guidance | Deschênes and da Silva synthesize teaching/learning recommenders; Lahoud directly evaluates major/career guidance. | Deschênes explicitly excludes pathway recommendations; da Silva’s review is teaching/learning and only 16 studies met its criteria. Lahoud uses historical graduate ratings, ontology/case-based reasoning and collaborative filtering in Lebanon. | Do not transfer learning-resource accuracy to admissions. Treat Lahoud as a close but non-equivalent comparator. Describe the gap as a locally scoped integration/evaluation question, not a universal absence of prior systems. |
| Hybrid recommendation | Burke provides a taxonomy and restaurant-system experiments; Lahoud reports a data-driven educational/career hybrid; reviews show hybrid is common in educational recommenders. | Hybrid methods differ structurally and depend on evidence and data. Burke’s logs are a different domain; Lahoud’s 98%/95% outputs depend on its own data, criteria and labels. Da Silva’s selected set does not establish a general advantage. | WiseLanka’s eligibility-first grouping plus within-group frozen content ranking is a particular architecture. It is not a reproduction of Burke or Lahoud and has not been shown superior. |
| Rules/constraints vs collaborative/content-based methods | Constraint-based recommendation uses explicit requirements and domain knowledge; it avoids requiring a historical user-item matrix. | This literature does not establish the authority, freshness or completeness of any particular education rule. A formally correct rule can still encode stale/incorrect source data; absent information needs explicit semantics. | Ground eligibility in cited provider/UGC evidence; preserve `NOT_ASSESSED`; distinguish rule execution correctness from source correctness. Source provenance is an engineering control whose educational validity still needs audit. |
| TF–IDF vs semantic embeddings | Salton/Buckley supports weighted lexical retrieval; SBERT motivates sentence embeddings for semantic similarity; MiniLM supports model compression. | These sources evaluate different tasks. MiniLM paper is not a sentence-recommendation or education study. Embedding benchmark results do not imply better ranking for WiseLanka. Semantic scores are not calibrated admission or career probabilities. | Compare all three production modes on the same candidates/scenarios. Report orderings and latency descriptively; calculate nDCG only after independent, blinded relevance ratings. |
| Explanation vs trust, comprehension and effectiveness | Tintarev/Masthoff distinguishes explanation goals; Knijnenburg et al. separates system properties, perceptions and user outcomes. | A clear or persuasive explanation may not be accurate or improve decisions; satisfaction is not comprehension or trust. The explanation studies do not directly establish educational trust effects. | Evaluate comprehension with answer tasks, clarity/usefulness with self-report, and trust separately. Do not combine these constructs into one accuracy or quality score. |
| Local guidance/accessibility | NEC provides historical career-guidance context; UNICEF provides a large historical child digital landscape study; the 2025 Jayasiri/Jayalal study provides local visually impaired learner evidence; UGC/TVEC are official information sources. | These sources differ in date, population and purpose. NEC/UNICEF are historical; UNICEF concerns children/online safety; Jayasiri/Jayalal concerns a specific visually impaired student group; UGC/TVEC documents do not evaluate platform effectiveness. | Use each source only for its population and date. Interface language switches, automated scans and browser speech controls are not evidence of fluent multilingual accessibility. |

## Strongest support for WiseLanka design choices

1. **Formal constraints before relevance:** constraint-based recommender literature supplies a reason to use explicit domain constraints when feasibility matters; WiseLanka must separately validate the source and preserve unknown evidence.
2. **Separate ranking evidence from educational outcomes:** educational reviews report that much work focuses on predictive/algorithmic measures, with fewer studies evaluating learning or pedagogical effects. This supports the project’s separation of functional tests, ranking comparisons and human evaluation—not a claim of benefit.
3. **Use lexical and semantic methods as competing baselines:** retrieval and embedding literature justifies comparing methods, while task mismatch means their published metrics cannot adjudicate WiseLanka’s 11-programme catalogue.
4. **Explain system states, not just scores:** the explanation literature supports measuring multiple aims and warns that clarity, satisfaction, effectiveness and trust may differ. For WiseLanka, show rule evidence, unknowns, conditions and source links; test comprehension independently.
5. **Treat accessibility as an evaluated property:** WCAG provides criteria; local studies provide context but are population-limited. Fluent-language, screen-reader, voice and device checks remain actual evaluation tasks, not features inferred from a selector.

## Sri Lankan context and source cautions

- The NEC study is a historical source on guidance in general education. It should not be cited as current population-level evidence for A/L, vocational and early-career needs.
- UNICEF’s 2018 digital landscape report has substantial mixed-method coverage and reports pretested Sinhala/Tamil/English instruments, but its unit of analysis is children’s digital access and online safety. It is not a current adult usability study or proof that local educational interfaces should use a particular language design.
- Jayasiri and Jayalal (2025) is locally relevant but has serious internal reporting inconsistencies (sample size versus residual degrees of freedom; adjusted R² exceeding R²; unclear hypotheses). Treat its quantitative results as unverified until clarified; do not use its effect estimates in Chapter 2. Its purposive single-school sample also limits transfer.
- Hosshanthika (2022) concerns Sinhala-to-Tamil news translation by undergraduates, not educational interfaces. At most it supports careful human review of educational terminology; do not generalise its proportions to WiseLanka translations.
- UGC admissions handbooks and TVEC/CareerOne publications are primary/administrative sources for what those bodies publish, not independent evaluations. Cite precise edition/date/page/conditions. Current policy must be rechecked for the intake under discussion.
- CareerMe and CareerOne marketing/provider pages may document advertised features, but they cannot establish user outcomes or competitor effectiveness.

## Remaining evidence gaps

1. Obtain and critically read full text for da Silva et al., Burke (if only repository reprint is acceptable under library practice), Tintarev and Masthoff, and any other source whose methods are currently abstract-only.
2. Independently appraise Lahoud’s varying dataset counts, relevance labels, comparison fairness, participant instruments and external validity; obtain and read the whole paper and tables rather than repeating headline percentages.
3. Retrieve the TVEC CareerOne PDF and verify all claims to be used; use provider-facing descriptions only for feature scope.
4. Search current official Sri Lankan sources for education transitions, TVET progression, labour-market/career information and language-access policy. Record date, population, scope and limitations; do not turn provider catalogues into evidence of learner need.
5. Find and read primary work on education/career decision-support, uncertainty/missing evidence, data provenance and algorithmic bias in high-impact recommendation. The existing sources support related concepts but do not fully address source freshness or rule uncertainty.
6. Review primary multilingual/accessibility research relevant to Sinhala/Tamil, screen readers, code switching, web speech recognition and mobile access. Separate source-language support from translated interface text.
7. Seek an independent, methodologically transparent local accessibility source; the inspected 2025 study has reporting inconsistencies that prevent using its numerical results as strong evidence.
8. Confirm UWL Harvard/Cite Them Right conventions and the required rubric against the official module brief; this matrix does not authenticate institutional marking requirements.

## Source-level verification notes

Primary/authoritative pages checked during this stage include the publisher records and accessible texts for Deschênes, da Silva, Lahoud, Tintarev/Masthoff and Knijnenburg et al.; ACL Anthology for SBERT; NeurIPS proceedings for MiniLM; the University of Tampere repository for the cumulative-gain manuscript; the official NEC report, UNICEF report, W3C recommendation and Hrčak-hosted 2025 accessibility paper. Existing local registers remain the record for the hash-identified UGC handbook and project-specific evidence. Publisher metadata verifies citation identity; it does not by itself establish claim entailment.

**No source in this matrix establishes that WiseLanka improves recommendation relevance, trust, usability, accessibility or learner outcomes.** Those remain empirical questions for the approved evaluation.
